# Figure captions

Drop-in captions. Each figure has a `.pdf` (vector — use this for submission)
and a `.png` (600 dpi, for preview or Word). Generated by `make_paper_figures.py`;
release it with the telemetry so the figures are reproducible from the raw data.

These captions match the manuscript as revised, including the renumbering to order of first citation in the text. Figs. 2 and 4 in the previous numbering were regenerated:
Fig. 2 no longer carries the withdrawn "redistributes rather than adds" claim
and now reports the full sensitivity of the rank correlation; Fig. 4 reports
differences with confidence intervals instead of Welch *t* statistics. See
`ERRATA.md`, items 6–9.

---

**Fig. 1. Memory traffic, not arithmetic, governs latency on this platform.**
Ratios of 1280 to 640 for the unified detector — one model, one backbone, one
thread count, so no capacity confound is present. Quadrupling the input area
scales arithmetic exactly (4.00×), but last-level cache read misses grow 4.94×
and measured wall-clock latency lands at 4.37×, between the two. The model's
weights are 36.2 MiB against a 2 MiB shared L3 — a factor of eighteen — so
weights are never resident and are streamed from DRAM on every inference, while
activation tensors scale with input area and dominate the growth in traffic. A
cost model based on FLOPs systematically under-predicts wall time on this
hardware.

---

**Fig. 2. Per-image latency and energy, unified versus separate.** Mean ± SD
over eight trials per configuration, consecutive within one battery session, all
on battery under the performance governor with four ONNX Runtime intra-op
threads. For the separate configuration, per-image latency is the sum of the
leaf and pest passes, which is what a two-model deployment costs per frame. The
unified configuration reduces latency by 30.0% at 640 and 29.5% at 1280 — a mean
difference of 153.1 ms (95% CI 151.3–154.9) and 652.4 ms (645.0–659.7) — and
gross energy per image by 28.8% and 29.0%, a difference of 1.77 J (1.72–1.81)
and 7.59 J (7.42–7.75). Differences are reported with confidence intervals
rather than test statistics because within-configuration dispersion is below
0.6% of the mean and the trials are not independent in the sense a t-test
assumes (see ERRATA.md, item 8). No throttling event occurred in any of the 32
trials. Because cross-model NMS adds computation on top of two full passes,
these figures understate the unified detector's advantage at the fair operating
point.

**Fig. 3. The classes that gain from higher input resolution are the smaller
ones, and they carry most of the data.** Change in AP@0.5 from 640 to 1280 for
the unified detector, against mean bounding-box area (log scale), for all twelve
classes. Six classes gain and six lose; the gaining set has a mean target area
of 4,016 px² against 76,794 px² for the losing set, and carries 1,527 of the
1,765 validation instances against 238. Because a macro-average weighs a
20-instance class as heavily as a 539-instance class, the gains and losses
appear to cancel and aggregate mAP@0.5 moves by only +0.007; weighted by
instance, mean AP rises from 0.436 to 0.520. Rank correlation between target
area and AP gain: Spearman ρ = −0.72 (p = 0.008) across all twelve,
ρ = −0.70 (p = 0.017) excluding weevil, and ρ = −0.60 (p = 0.067) excluding
both classes declared under-sampled a priori, which does not reach significance
at n = 10; excluding both also reduces the area ratio from 19× to 9.7×. The
trend is reported as directional evidence consistent with the mechanism rather
than as an independently significant finding. Stem_borer departs from it: it has
the smallest mean area in the set yet loses 0.019, because its AP is dominated
by missed detections against background rather than by localisation error.
Squares mark the two classes declared under-sampled in validation a priori
(weevil, 4 instances; Pink_disease, 20); they are plotted for completeness and
not interpreted individually.

**Fig. 4. Instrumentation and measurement node.** Power is sampled at the
battery pack (13.6–16.7 V), upstream of the HAT's 5 V buck converter, so the
reported draw includes the SoC, the Active Cooler, the HAT's quiescent
consumption, and buck conversion loss. This is the node that sets field
endurance, and it is not the node at which published bare-board figures are
measured. The gauge is characterised by observable behaviour rather than by
part number: the four cell voltages sum to the reported pack voltage to within
1 mV, and remaining capacity divided by current reproduces the reported
run-time-to-empty (211 vs. 212 min), indicating coulomb counting rather than
voltage lookup. A discharge control experiment bounds the confound from pack
state of charge at 0.35% of active power under fixed CPU load.
