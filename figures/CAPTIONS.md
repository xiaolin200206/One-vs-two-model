# Figure captions

Drop-in captions. Each figure has a `.pdf` (vector — use this for submission)
and a `.png` (600 dpi, for preview or Word). Generated by `make_figs.py`;
release it with the telemetry so the figures are reproducible from the raw data.

---

**Fig. 1. Instrumentation and measurement node.** Power is sampled at the
battery pack (13.6–16.7 V), upstream of the HAT's 5 V buck converter, so the
reported draw includes the SoC, the Active Cooler, the HAT's quiescent
consumption, and buck conversion loss. This is the node that sets field
endurance, and it is not the node at which published bare-board figures are
measured. The gauge is characterised by observable behaviour rather than by
part number: the four cell voltages sum to the reported pack voltage to within
1 mV, and remaining capacity divided by current reproduces the reported
run-time-to-empty (211 vs. 212 min), indicating coulomb counting rather than
voltage lookup. A discharge control experiment bounds the confound from pack
state of charge at 0.35% of active power under fixed CPU load.

---

**Fig. 2. Raising input resolution redistributes accuracy across target sizes
rather than adding it.** Change in AP@0.5 from 640 to 1280 for the unified
detector, against mean bounding-box area (log scale), for all twelve classes.
Six classes gain and six lose; the gaining set has a mean target area of
4,016 px² against 76,794 px² for the losing set. Spearman ρ = −0.72 (p = 0.008)
across all twelve, ρ = −0.70 (p = 0.017) excluding weevil. Aggregate mAP@0.5
moves by +0.007 because the gains and losses cancel. Stem_borer departs from
the trend: it has the smallest mean area in the set yet loses 0.019, because
its AP is dominated by missed detections against background rather than by
localisation error. Squares mark the two classes declared under-sampled in
validation a priori (weevil, 4 instances; Pink_disease, 20); they are plotted
for completeness and not interpreted individually.

---

**Fig. 3. Memory traffic, not arithmetic, governs latency on this platform.**
Ratios of 1280 to 640 for the unified detector — one model, one backbone, one
thread count, so no capacity confound is present. Quadrupling the input area
scales arithmetic exactly (4.00×), but last-level cache read misses grow 4.94×
and measured wall-clock latency lands at 4.37×, between the two. The model's
weights are 36.2 MiB against a 2 MiB shared L3 — a factor of eighteen — so
weights are never resident and are streamed from DRAM on every inference, while
activation tensors scale with input area and dominate the growth in traffic. A
cost model based on FLOPs systematically under-predicts wall time on this
hardware.

---

**Fig. 4. Per-image latency and energy, unified versus separate.** Mean ± SD
over eight independent trials per configuration, all on battery under the
performance governor with four ONNX Runtime intra-op threads. For the separate
configuration, per-image latency is the sum of the leaf and pest passes, which
is what a two-model deployment costs per frame. The unified configuration
reduces latency by 30.0% at 640 (Welch's t = −185.1, p = 8.4 × 10⁻²³) and 29.5%
at 1280 (t = −195.6, p = 5.5 × 10⁻²¹), and gross energy per image by 28.8% and
29.0%. Latency standard deviation is below 0.6% of the mean for every
configuration. No throttling event occurred in any of the 32 trials. Because
cross-model NMS adds computation on top of two full passes, these figures
understate the unified detector's advantage at the fair operating point.
